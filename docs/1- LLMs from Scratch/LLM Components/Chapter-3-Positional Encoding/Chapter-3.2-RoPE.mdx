# Sinusoidal Positional Encoding

## 1. What is it?

Sinusoidal positional encoding is a way to give a Transformer information about the **position of each token**.

Unlike learned positional embeddings, the positional values are **not learned**.

They are generated using fixed mathematical functions:

$$  
\boxed{  
PE(pos,2i)=\sin\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d}}\right)  
}  
$$

$$  
\boxed{  
PE(pos,2i+1)=\cos\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d}}\right)  
}  
$$

Where:
- $pos$ = position of the token
- $i$ = dimension-pair index
- $d$ = embedding dimension

---

# 2. Example

Suppose our sentence has 5 tokens:

```text
I   am   very   cool   today
```

Positions:

|Token|Position|
|---|--:|
|I|0|
|am|1|
|very|2|
|cool|3|
|today|4|

Assume each token has a **4-dimensional embedding**:

$$  
d=4  
$$

---

# 3. Token Embeddings

For example:

$$  
X=  
\begin{bmatrix}  
0.43 & 0.15 & 0.89 & 0.22 \\  
0.55 & 0.87 & 0.66 & 0.31 \\ 
0.57 & 0.85 & 0.64 & 0.44 \\  
0.22 & 0.58 & 0.33 & 0.72 \\  
0.77 & 0.25 & 0.10 & 0.63  
\end{bmatrix}  
$$

Each row represents one token.

---

# 4. Calculate the frequency denominator

The sinusoidal formula contains:

$$  
10000^{2i/d}  
$$
For:
$$  
d=4  
$$

we have:
$$  
i=0,1  
$$
because each $i$ controls a pair of dimensions.
### For $i=0$

$$  
10000^{2(0)/4} = 10000^0 = 1  
$$

### For $i=1$

$$  
10000^{2(1)/4} = 10000^{1/2} = 100  
$$

Therefore:

$$  
\boxed{  
[1,;100]  
}  
$$

These are the denominators used by the two frequency pairs.

---

# 5. Why two values?

Our embedding has 4 dimensions:

$$  
[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]  
$$

The sinusoidal encoding uses:

```text
dimension 0 → sin using denominator 1
dimension 1 → cos using denominator 1

dimension 2 → sin using denominator 100
dimension 3 → cos using denominator 100
```

So:

$$  
PE(pos)=  
[  
\sin(pos/1),  
\cos(pos/1),  
\sin(pos/100),  
\cos(pos/100)  
]  
$$

---

# 6. Position 0

For:
$$  
pos=0  
$$
we get:

$$  
PE(0)=  
[  
\sin(0/1),  
\cos(0/1),  
\sin(0/100),  
\cos(0/100)  
]  
$$
Therefore:
$$  
PE(0)=  
[  
\sin(0),  
\cos(0),  
\sin(0),  
\cos(0)  
]  
$$

$$  
\boxed{  
PE(0)=[0,;1,;0,;1]  
}  
$$

---

# 7. Position 1

For:

$$  
pos=1  
$$

$$  
PE(1)=  
[  
\sin(1),  
\cos(1),  
\sin(1/100),  
\cos(1/100)  
]  
$$

Using:

$$  
\sin(1)\approx0.84147  
$$

$$  
\cos(1)\approx0.54030  
$$

$$  
\sin(0.01)\approx0.0099998  
$$

$$  
\cos(0.01)\approx0.99995  
$$

Therefore:

$$  
\boxed{  
PE(1)\approx  
[0.84147,;0.54030,;0.01000,;0.99995]  
}  
$$

---

# 8. Position 2

$$  
PE(2)=  
[  
\sin(2),  
\cos(2),  
\sin(2/100),  
\cos(2/100)  
]  
$$

Approximately:

$$  
\boxed{  
PE(2)\approx  
[0.90930,;-0.41615,;0.02000,;0.99980]

# 10. Position 4

$$  
PE(4)=  
[  
\sin(4),  
\cos(4),

]  
$$

$$  
\boxed{

---

# 11. Complete positional encoding matrix

Putting all positions together:

$$  
PE=  
\begin{bmatrix}  
0 & 1 & 0 & 1 \\  
0.84147 & 0.54030 & 0.01000 & 0.99995 \\  
0.90930 & -0.41615 & 0.02000 & 0.99980 \\  
0.14112 & -0.98999 & 0.03000 & 0.99955 \\  
-0.75680 & -0.65364 & 0.03999 & 0.99920  
\end{bmatrix}  
$$

Notice:
**This matrix was not learned.**
It was calculated directly from the mathematical formulas.

---
# 12. Add positional encoding to token embeddings
The Transformer adds the two matrices element-by-element:
$$  
X_{\text{final}}=

X_{\text{token}}  
+  
PE  
$$

Token embeddings:

$$  
\begin{bmatrix}  
0.43 & 0.15 & 0.89 & 0.22 \\  
0.55 & 0.87 & 0.66 & 0.31 \\  
0.57 & 0.85 & 0.64 & 0.44 \\  
0.22 & 0.58 & 0.33 & 0.72 \\  
0.77 & 0.25 & 0.10 & 0.63  
\end{bmatrix}  
$$

Positional encoding:
$$  
+  
\begin{bmatrix}  
0 & 1 & 0 & 1 \\  
0.84147 & 0.54030 & 0.01000 & 0.99995 \\  
0.90930 & -0.41615 & 0.02000 & 0.99980 \\  
0.14112 & -0.98999 & 0.03000 & 0.99955 \\  
-0.75680 & -0.65364 & 0.03999 & 0.99920  
\end{bmatrix}  
$$
Result:

$$  
\boxed{  
\begin{bmatrix}  
0.43 & 1.15 & 0.89 & 1.22 \\  
1.39147 & 1.41030 & 0.67000 & 1.30995 \\  
1.47930 & 0.43385 & 0.66000 & 1.43980 \\  
0.36112 & -0.40999 & 0.36000 & 1.71955 \\  
0.01320 & -0.40364 & 0.13999 & 1.62920  
\end{bmatrix}  
}  
$$

This resulting matrix is what gets passed to the next part of the Transformer.

---

# 13. Flow

```text
Position
   ↓
mathematical formula
   ↓
different sin/cos values
   ↓
positional vector
   ↓
add to token embedding
```

---

# 14. Fast vs slow frequencies

The first pair changes quickly:
$$  
\sin(pos)  ,\cos(pos)  
$$
The second pair changes slowly:
$$  
\sin(pos/100)  ,\cos(pos/100)  
$$

# BPTT: Time Traveling Mistakes

Now that we have unrolled our RNN into a "comic strip" that lays time out flat, we can finally train our AI using a technique called **BPTT** (Backpropagation Through Time). 

Despite the cool sci-fi name, BPTT is just regular backpropagation, but with a time-travel twist.

---

## The Assembly Line Analogy

Imagine a car factory assembly line with 5 stations:
1. Station 1 builds the frame.
2. Station 2 adds the engine.
3. Station 3 adds the wheels.
4. Station 4 adds the doors.
5. Station 5 paints the car.

At the very end of the line, the boss inspects the car and realizes the engine is completely crooked! The car is ruined. 

How does the boss fix this? He doesn't just yell at the painter at Station 5. The painter didn't touch the engine! 
Instead, the boss walks **backwards** down the assembly line. 
- He walks past Station 5...
- He walks past Station 4...
- He walks past Station 3...
- He gets to Station 2, finds the guy who installed the engine, and tells him to adjust his tools!

<div style={{backgroundColor: '#fffbe6', padding: '10px', borderRadius: '5px'}}>
<strong>BPTT in AI:</strong> When the RNN makes a bad prediction at the very end of a sentence, the AI calculates the error (the yelling boss). It then takes that error and passes it <em>backwards through time</em> (backwards through the unrolled comic strip), adjusting the weights at every single step!
</div>

## The Shared Brain Rule

Here is where BPTT gets a little weird compared to the factory. 

In the factory, there are 5 different workers. 
But remember our Golden Rule from the last section? In an unrolled RNN, **it's the exact same network at every step.** It's like one super-fast worker running down the assembly line, doing every job himself!

Because of this, when the error signal travels backward from Step 5 to Step 1, it is collecting adjustments for the *exact same set of weights* over and over again. 

The AI adds up all these adjustments together:
`Total Adjustment = (Mistake at Step 5) + (Mistake at Step 4) + (Mistake at Step 3)...`

Once the error reaches the very beginning of time (Step 1), the AI takes that massive, combined adjustment and finally updates the actual brain of the network!

## The Problem with Time Travel

Sending error signals backwards through time works perfectly for short sentences (short assembly lines). But what if the assembly line is 1,000 steps long? 

Walking an error backwards through 1,000 steps causes the math to break down in spectacular fashion.

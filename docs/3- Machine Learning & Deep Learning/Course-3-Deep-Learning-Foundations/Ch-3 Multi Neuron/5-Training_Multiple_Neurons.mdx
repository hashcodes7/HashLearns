# Phase 5 — Multiple Neurons

## 5.5 Training Multiple Neurons

A layer with multiple neurons has multiple weights and biases.

$$
y=Wx+b
$$

For 2 neurons and 3 inputs:

$$
W=
\begin{bmatrix}
w_{11}&w_{12}&w_{13}\\
w_{21}&w_{22}&w_{23}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Each weight can be updated independently.

---

## 5.6 Error and Loss

Suppose the layer produces:

$$
y=
\begin{bmatrix}
y_1\\
y_2
\end{bmatrix}
$$

and the target is:

$$
t=
\begin{bmatrix}
t_1\\
t_2
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The error for each output is:

$$
error_1=t_1-y_1
$$

$$
error_2=t_2-y_2
$$

Using squared loss:

$$
L_1=(t_1-y_1)^2
$$

$$
L_2=(t_2-y_2)^2
$$

Total loss:

$$
\boxed{L=L_1+L_2}
$$

---

## 5.7 Updating the Weights

Each neuron has its own gradients.

For neuron 1:

$$
\frac{dL}{dw_{11}}=-2x_1(error_1)
$$

$$
\frac{dL}{dw_{12}}=-2x_2(error_1)
$$

For neuron 2:

$$
\frac{dL}{dw_{21}}=-2x_1(error_2)
$$

$$
\frac{dL}{dw_{22}}=-2x_2(error_2)
$$

The same gradient-descent rule applies:

$$
\boxed{parameter=parameter-\eta\times gradient}
$$

So multiple neurons simply mean more parameters to update.

---

## 5.8 Python Example

```python
import numpy as np

x = np.array([2, 3])
target = np.array([10, 15])

W = np.array([
    [1.0, 1.0],
    [1.0, 1.0]
])

b = np.array([0.0, 0.0])

learning_rate = 0.01

for step in range(100):
    prediction = W @ x + b
    error = target - prediction
    loss = np.sum(error ** 2)

    gradient_W = -2 * np.outer(error, x)
    gradient_b = -2 * error

    W = W - learning_rate * gradient_W
    b = b - learning_rate * gradient_b

print("prediction:", prediction)
print("loss:", loss)
print("W:", W)
print("b:", b)
```

---

## Key Idea

For one neuron:

$$
y=w^Tx+b
$$

For multiple neurons:

$$
\boxed{y=Wx+b}
$$

During training, every weight and bias receives its own gradient.

$$
\boxed{W=W-\eta\frac{\partial L}{\partial W}}
$$

$$
\boxed{b=b-\eta\frac{\partial L}{\partial b}}
$$

# 4- Matrix Multiplication — Dimensions

Before multiplying matrices, we need to know whether their dimensions are compatible.

---

## 4.7 Matrix Dimensions

A matrix is described by:

$$
\boxed{\text{rows}\times\text{columns}}
$$

For example:

$$
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2&3\\
4&5&6
\end{bmatrix}
$$

has:

$$
\boxed{2\times3}
$$

It has **2 rows** and **3 columns**.

---

## 4.8 Multiplication Rule

Suppose:

$$
A_{m\times n}
$$

and:

$$
B_{n\times p}
$$

Then:

$$
\boxed{A_{m\times n}B_{n\times p}=C_{m\times p}}
$$

The important rule is:

$$
\boxed{\text{columns of }A=\text{rows of }B}
$$

The dimensions on the inside must match.

---

## 4.9 Example

Let:

$$
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&2&3\\
4&5&6
\end{bmatrix}
$$

So:

$$
A:2\times3
$$

and:

$$
B=
\begin{bmatrix}
7&8\\
9&10\\
11&12
\end{bmatrix}
$$

So:

$$
B:3\times2
$$

The multiplication is valid:

$$
\boxed{(2\times3)(3\times2)}
$$

because:

$$
3=3
$$

The result has the outer dimensions:

$$
\boxed{2\times2}
$$

---

## 4.10 Why This Matters for Neural Networks

Suppose we have:

* 3 inputs
* 2 neurons

The weight matrix is:

$$
W:2\times3
$$

The input vector is:

$$
x:3\times1
$$

Therefore:

$$
Wx=(2\times3)(3\times1)
$$

which gives:

$$
\boxed{2\times1}
$$

The result contains one output for each neuron.

---

## Key Idea

For:

$$
A_{m\times n}B_{n\times p}
$$

the result is:

$$
\boxed{C_{m\times p}}
$$

Remember:

$$
\boxed{\text{inside dimensions must match}}
$$

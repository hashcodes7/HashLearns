# 4. Backpropagation

Backpropagation means calculating:

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{d\text{parameter}}}
$$

for every weight and bias.

We start at the loss and move backward.

```text
L
↓
y
↓
a1, a2, a3
↓
z1, z2, z3
↓
hidden weights and biases
```

The chain rule is the main mechanism.

---

# 5. Start at the Loss

Our loss is:

$$
L=(target-y)^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{dL}{dy}
=
-2(target-y)
$$

Here:

$$
target=20
$$

and:

$$
y=34
$$

So:

$$
\frac{dL}{dy}
=
-2(20-34)
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{dy}=28}
$$

The positive gradient tells us that increasing the prediction increases the loss at this point.
The final neuron is:

$$
y=w_1a_1+w_2a_2+w_3a_3+b
$$

We need gradients for:

$$
w_1,w_2,w_3,b
$$

## Gradient of w1

$$
\frac{dy}{dw_1}=a_1
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{dL}{dw_1}
=
\frac{dL}{dy}
\frac{dy}{dw_1}
$$

$$
=28(9)
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{dw_1}=252}
$$

## Gradient of w2

$$
\frac{dL}{dw_2}
=
\frac{dL}{dy}a_2
$$

$$
=28(9)
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{dw_2}=252}
$$

## Gradient of w3

$$
\frac{dL}{dw_3}
=
\frac{dL}{dy}a_3
$$

$$
=28(6)
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{dw_3}=168}
$$

## Gradient of final bias

Because:

$$
\frac{dy}{db}=1
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{db}=28}
$$

---

# 7. Continue Backward Through the Hidden Layer

Now we need gradients for:

$$
a_1,a_2,a_3
$$

For $a_1$:

$$
\frac{dy}{da_1}=w_1
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{dL}{da_1}
=
\frac{dL}{dy}
\frac{dy}{da_1}
$$

$$
=28(1)
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{da_1}=28}
$$

For $a_2$:

$$
\frac{dL}{da_2}=28(2)
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{da_2}=56}
$$

For $a_3$:

$$
\frac{dL}{da_3}=28(1)
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{da_3}=28}
$$

---

# 8. Backpropagate Through ReLU

ReLU is:

$$
a=ReLU(z)=\max(0,z)
$$

Its derivative is:

$$
\frac{da}{dz}
=
\begin{cases}
1 & z>0\\
0 & z<0
\end{cases}
$$

Our values are:

$$
z_1=9,\quad z_2=9,\quad z_3=6
$$

All are positive.

Therefore:

$$
\frac{da_1}{dz_1}=1
$$

$$
\frac{da_2}{dz_2}=1
$$

$$
\frac{da_3}{dz_3}=1
$$

So:

$$
\frac{dL}{dz_1}
=
\frac{dL}{da_1}
\frac{da_1}{dz_1}
=
28(1)
=
28
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{dz_1}=28}
$$

Similarly:

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{dz_2}=56}
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{dz_3}=28}
$$

---

# 9. Backpropagate Into Hidden Weights

Neuron 1:

$$
z_1=w_{11}x_1+w_{12}x_2+b_1
$$

For $w_{11}$:

$$
\frac{dz_1}{dw_{11}}=x_1
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{dL}{dw_{11}}
=
\frac{dL}{dz_1}
\frac{dz_1}{dw_{11}}
$$

$$
=28(2)
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{dw_{11}}=56}
$$

For $w_{12}$:

$$
\frac{dL}{dw_{12}}
=
28(3)
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{dw_{12}}=84}
$$

For $b_1$:

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{db_1}=28}
$$

---

## Neuron 2

We have:

$$
\frac{dL}{dz_2}=56
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{dw_{21}}=56(2)=112}
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{dw_{22}}=56(3)=168}
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{db_2}=56}
$$

---

## Neuron 3

We have:

$$
\frac{dL}{dz_3}=28
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{dw_{31}}=28(2)=56}
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{dw_{32}}=28(3)=84}
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{dL}{db_3}=28}
$$

---

# 11. Weight and Bias Update

Backpropagation gave us the gradients.

Now gradient descent uses them to change the parameters.

The update rule is:

$$
\boxed{
parameter_{new}
=
parameter_{old}
-
\eta
\frac{dL}{d(parameter)}
}
$$

where $\eta$ is the learning rate.

Use:

```python
learning_rate = 0.001
```

For example:

$$
w_1^{new}
=
1-0.001(252)
$$

$$
\boxed{w_1^{new}=0.748}
$$

For $w_2$:

$$
w_2^{new}=2-0.001(252)
$$

$$
\boxed{w_2^{new}=1.748}
$$

For $b$:

$$
b^{new}=1-0.001(28)
$$

$$
\boxed{b^{new}=0.972}
$$

The same update is performed for every parameter.
